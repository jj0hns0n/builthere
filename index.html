Your business is a system of systems

Nobody designed your business. It accumulated. QuickBooks came in when the shoebox stopped working. The scheduling software arrived with the third crew. Someone built the spreadsheet that holds the parts neither of those covers. Payroll went to a service, the estimates live in a folder, and the real customer history is in one person's inbox. Every one of those was the right call at the time, and every one of them does its own job well.

What nobody bought, and nobody owns, is the space between them.

That space is where the business actually runs, and it's where your problems live. The retyping. The two reports that don't agree. The job that got finished and never invoiced. The simple question (Did we invoice everything we delivered? Which customers are quietly fading? Why doesn't the inventory count match?) that takes a day to answer because the answer is split across three systems that have never been in the same room.

Your books know a job made money. They don't know it was the big one across town, sold by a particular person, with three change orders, that sat six weeks between finished and invoiced. Anything that reads one system is answering half a question, no matter how good it is at the half it can see.

So the problem is almost never the software you bought. It's that you bought seven of them, and the only thing connecting them is people.

The thing that changed

Businesses have had this problem forever. What's new is that it finally became cheap to look at all of it at once.

Every system you own sees its own slice, by design. Your accountant sees the ledger. Your operations software sees the schedule. A consultant can hold part of it in their head for the length of an engagement, at consultant rates, and then it walks out the door with them. Nobody has ever been able to sit down and read the whole business, in detail, for a price a small business would pay.

That is what AI actually changes. A model can read your ledger, your job records, your inboxes, and your spreadsheets in one sitting and reason across them. Not summarize each one separately: connect them. This invoice, that job, that email thread, that payment, as one picture. Ask why the margin slipped on a kind of work you do, and it can go look at every job you've run, in every system at once, and tell you what the bad ones had in common.

Understanding it is the cheap half now. The half that matters is what you do about it, and the answer is almost always to put a system in: the connection built between two things that never talked, the reconciliation that runs itself, the reply already drafted when you open your mail, the monthly number that lands on your desk without anyone compiling it. A report tells you where the business is leaking. A system stops the leak.

What that looks like in practice

Not a chatbot on your website. Real work:

  • Answer questions that span all of it. Your books, your operations system, your spreadsheets, connected and queryable in plain English. "Show me every customer who bought last spring but hasn't this year."
  • Do the retyping. Invoices entered, orders keyed between systems, reports assembled. The routine transfer work that eats an afternoon, done by software while your people do the job you actually hired them for.
  • Work every inbox at once. Shared mailboxes and personal ones, connected so one assistant can search across all of them. "Find every quote request from last month that nobody answered." No forwarding rules, no logging in one at a time, no wondering where it landed.
  • Draft the replies that repeat. Quote requests, order status, proposals, follow-ups. It reads the thread, pulls the numbers off your price list and your order history, and leaves a reply waiting in drafts in your voice. You read it and hit send.
  • Catch what leaks. Reconciling across systems finds the shipment that was never invoiced, the commission that was never collected, the stock count that can't be right. In a small business, those leaks are real dollars.
  • Watch the numbers every month. Control reports that run themselves: what changed, what doesn't add up, what needs a decision. On your desk without anyone spending a day compiling it.

One rule that never bends: AI drafts words; code does arithmetic. Any number you or your customers will rely on (a price, a payout, a reconciled balance) is computed by deterministic, auditable software. A language model never invents a dollar figure.

Already using AI, and stuck?

Maybe this is you instead. You didn't wait around. You pay for Claude or ChatGPT, you use it every day, and you've gone past chatting with it: projects and custom instructions, a few automations that run on their own, maybe a Zapier flow or two. You've seen enough to know there's more here for your business.

And now you're stuck at the same place everyone gets stuck: it can't reach your stuff. Not your company email, not your books, not your order history. There's a list of connectors, and none of them are for the systems your business actually runs on. So you keep pasting things in by hand, the automation breaks the moment an invoice looks slightly different, and the gap between "this is impressive" and "this reliably runs part of my business" turns out to be wide.

That wall is real, and it isn't a you problem. Getting past it is engineering: building the connector that plugs your own systems into the AI you're already paying for, putting guardrails around what it's allowed to decide, and adding the boring, dependable plumbing that consumer tools don't come with. You keep working in the same app you already know. It just gets its hands on your business. That's exactly the work I do, and the fact that you've already pushed this far means we skip the sales pitch and start where you left off.

The work usually starts small and unglamorous. Give the assistant real access to every company mailbox at once instead of one at a time. Then have it read the order history. Then have it draft the replies that repeat. Each piece is boring on its own, and together they're the difference between a tool you chat with and one that runs part of the business.
